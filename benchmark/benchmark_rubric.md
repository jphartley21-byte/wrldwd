# Benchmark rubric: what the original does, how to score a draft against it

Inputs merged: original_technique_map.md (line-by-line from the lyrics Joe pasted), genius_and_reviews_findings.md (Genius annotations, reviews, production), reaction_findings.md (17 reaction videos), comedy_theory.md (Joe's definition plus newer evidence).
Scoring per criterion: 0 = absent, 1 = weak, 2 = solid, 3 = matches the original. Targets are what the original does (numbers from my counts: 76 lines in the first pass, about 9.3 syllables per line, about one line per bar at 140 BPM).
A draft is judged only on technique and craft. The original's lyrics are never to be copied or templated.
**REV 2 (9 Oct 2026): Joe says "there's actually not really any jokes at all, the comedy comes from the characters and wordplay", with the rhyme scheme and flow selling both. Section I below is authoritative; where criteria 3, 12, 16, 18, 33-37 conflict with it, section I wins (they have been reworded here).**

## A. Structure and flow
1. **Chain length and coverage.** Verse is built from long monorhyme chains. Original: chains of 8, 8, 8, 13, 16, 4, 4 lines; about 92% of verse lines inside a chain; longest run 15 lines on one sound with one break. 1 = couplets or 4-line blocks. 2 = runs of 6-8, about 75% coverage. 3 = at least one run of 12+ and about 90% coverage.
2. **Weak-syllable endings.** Rhymes land on unstressed tails (stressed syllable plus a soft one, plus a tiny word like it/in/him), so they never sound like nursery rhymes. Original: one 8-line dactyl block, one 7-line "-al" block, an 11-line "-ee" block with the stressed vowel drifting. 3 = at least one 8-line block of 3-syllable endings and most other chains on soft endings.
3. **Breath lines (X lines).** One short, plain, non-rhyming line inside a long chain, a breath before the chain resumes (NOT a gag setup). Original: about one per 8-12 lines (4 in the verse). 3 = every long chain has one breath line, short and unremarkable.
4. **Hinge line.** The last line of a block already carries the next block's sound. Original: at least one clear hinge, and chains continue across section breaks. 3 = at least two hinges.
5. **Line-length variance.** Original: 20% of lines 7 syllables or fewer, 14% 12 or more, mean 9.3, shapes like short-short-short-LONG. 3 = within a few points of those percentages, and shapes visible in the draft.
6. **Escalation arc and bookend.** The persona stays nonchalant while the scale of what is reported grows, then drops into leisure, then ends on a fast montage; the hook returns unchanged at the end. 3 = clear arc and bookend.

## B. Rhyme craft
7. **Internal rhyme and vowel carpets.** About two rhymes per bar in dense blocks (one mid-bar, one at the end) and runs of one vowel through whole blocks. 3 = at least 60% of lines in dense blocks carry an internal rhyme.
8. **Cut-up, mosaic and homophone rhymes.** Sounds that match across different word splits. Original: at least five (plain/plane pair, lonesome/long one, double it/shovel it, apologise/fraternise, Dubai/doobies). 3 = at least four in the draft, each natural, none forced.
9. **Enjambed rhyme.** Lines ending on a pronoun, or a word or acronym split across the bar line, so the rhyme lands before the sentence ends. Original: two "I"-ended lines and one split acronym. 3 = at least two.
10. **Reduplicated percussive words.** dot-dot, ping-pong, click-clack, naughty-naughty. Original: at least six. 3 = at least four natural ones.
11. **Choose names for their vowel.** Place names are picked because they fit the chain's sound (the chorus places share one long vowel). 3 = place names in the hook share a vowel family, and verse places are matched to chain sounds.

## C. Content and character
12. **Nonchalance density (character).** [replaces "Joke density"] Share of bars that report something big, illicit, dangerous or absurd in a flat errand or domestic voice, often addressed to an ordinary person in the narrator's life, with no punchline and no wink. Model (Joe): the original's line about being away in a rich desert city smoking weed and sending word to his wife that he'll be back shortly. Original (Genius pass): C and CW lines are about a third of its unique lines (27 of 81), usually 2-4 line runs with a bland setup line and a bathetic closer on the rhyme beat; extraordinary acts (killing, bribing, trafficking, smuggling) are narrated in everyday British admin or consumer register (parcel delivery, a pub chain, a schoolboy telling-off, cigarette prices); the domestic third party is mentioned, not addressed. 3 = about a third of the verse bars are nonchalant reports, in at least three different frames (message, errand, understatement), none cloned from the original's frames.
13. **Echo with a twist.** A word is repeated with a changed meaning, and idioms made literal (slap on the wrist becomes slapped a fifty; lights out becomes a bulb). Original: at least seven. 3 = at least five, each paying off.
14. **Number ladder.** A counted run of rhyme words that escalates (thirty, forty, fifty). 3 = at least one 3-step ladder.
15. **Lexical-set metaphors.** One domain's vocabulary describes another (logistics for dealing; sport for violence; music for guns; drinks). For us: travel words with a criminal second meaning (connections, booked, held up, flight risk, travel light). 3 = at least two sets running through the song.
16. **Domestic anchors in the voice.** [replaces "Bathos from British mundanity"] Ordinary British objects and obligations (tea, bins, rent, parking, a pub chain, a telling-off, a parcel, Mum, the lads, the landlord) carry the nonchalance as the register the extraordinary thing is reported in. They are the narrator's voice, not a comedown and not itinerary logistics. Third parties are usually MENTIONED, not addressed (the original never addresses the wife; its narrator talks to the audience, his mates or nobody). 3 = a domestic anchor in about a third of the bars, each concrete and true-sounding.
17. **Understated menace, profanity-free.** Euphemism, kids' games and sports carry the violence; no swearing; the tone never changes. Joe has allowed cartoonish drug and violence imagery, and mild swearing only. 3 = the menace is in the euphemism.
18. **Self-mockery (optional, only if true).** The original's rappers joke about their own looks and age inside the boast. Ours may do this only with facts Joe supplies or confirms (never invented looks or age). 3 = at least two true self-mocking details Joe approves.
19. **Roll-call hook with a local word per place.** Each stop gets one real local word or cliche; frame reuse ("just + verb + place") moves scenes in the verse. The title is said once, in a hinge; the chorus never says it. 3 = a hook of 12 or more bars built this way, with a local word per place and the title held back.
20. **Voice tics as rhythm.** "mate", "then", "nah", "Lord knows" dropped mid-bar as filler and breath. 3 = tics at fixed rhythm positions, not decoration.

## D. Delivery and sound (for Suno)
21. **Two voices.** A rougher voice and a deeper voice, with some words handed from one to the other. 3 = tags or structure that give two distinct deliveries, or a single deadpan voice with a deliberate gruff/deep split.
22. **Hook riff and beat.** One catchy, instantly recognisable melodic riff in the beat, tempo 140-142, drill feel. Joe's AI analysis and the tempo sources disagree on key (G minor versus D major / B minor), so the style prompt should leave the key vague or let Joe choose.
23. **Breath-control bursts.** Some lines (12 to 15 syllables) are packed in one bar; short lines punch. 3 = bursts placed at block openings.

## E. Hygiene (pass/fail)
24. **No AI-tell words or shapes.** No neon, echoes, whispers, shadows, tapestry, journey, grind, king/throne, rule-of-three lists, abstract nouns, moralising endings, explained jokes. Tidy symmetric AABB quatrains count as a fail.
25. **No copying.** No run of four words or distinctive phrase from the original; no cloning of line templates (for example greeting plus "to the boys in" plus a place; "tell her I'm out in X doing Y, back shortly").
26. **True or flagged.** Every claim maps to a row in travel_history.md or is flagged "invented" or "(persona)" and approved by Joe. No hobby/nerd material.

## F. Added from listener reactions (17 reaction videos and the official video's top comments, plus 6 transcripts re-read; see reaction_findings.md and comedy_theory.md)
27. **Signature set-piece.** One 8-10 bar double-time run of dense multisyllabic wordplay on a single sound, the most-quoted moment of the song (the "Dublin / double it" passage was the most-cited bar across reactors). 3 = one such run, clearly the peak of the draft, placed after a slower stretch.
28. **Layers that reward a rewind.** Lines with two readings that a first listen misses (homophone, network-not-social, tonic wine plus a pun, a word handed across two voices). 3 = at least six lines that work on two levels.
29. **Handoff device.** A word, rhyme or acronym handed from one voice to the other across the bar line, plus an unusually long back-to-back. 3 = at least one handoff that completes only when the second voice lands.
30. **Accuracy of the local layer.** Reactors from the places named confirmed the references as accurate and culturally deep; wrong or touristy slang would have sunk it. 3 = every foreign or slang word is correct, used by someone who has been there, and Joe confirms.
31. **Lived detail.** Reviewers say the persona works because the ordinary details feel earned (cigarette prices, a bribe, a haircut). 3 = at least five details that only Joe's real life and trips could have produced (from travel_history.md and his stories).

Listener ranking of what matters most (use as weights when two criteria compete): (1) layered wordplay that rewards rewinds; (2) deadpan persona against expectations; (3) flow and two contrasting voices; (4) the double-time set-piece; (5) chemistry and handoffs; (6) a sustained worldwide concept with accurate references; (7) a party-ready beat built on one familiar-feeling riff; (8) authenticity. Hard to replicate by design: deadpan timing, accent-driven rhymes, slang layers, decades of lived detail, two distinct voices trading, and the performers' look (we are audio only).

## G. Flow and cringe (added on Joe's feedback, 9 Oct 2026: early bars were "lame, cringe and don't flow")
32. **Scansion and speakability.** Each bar has four stressed beats at 142 BPM with the rhyme on the last stress (beat four); no stress clashes or tongue-twisting clusters; every bar can be said in one breath; the line parses without needing a colon or a pause to be understood. Marked as stress pattern in capitals during editing. 3 = every bar scans, short bars are deliberate, bursts are triplet-style and still clear.
33. **Register.** First-person, conversational narration with verbs, 'I', 'so', 'then', 'just': a bloke talking. Noun-fragment lists, colon-led stand-up one-liners and sentence fragments are allowed only as a deliberate short breath line. 3 = at least 80% of bars are full spoken clauses.
34. **Cringe filter (fail-level).** No groaner puns (top banana, in the pink, de Nile, any port in a storm, so aisle be back), no puns that announce themselves, no explained jokes, no try-hard slang, no invented characters or personal anecdotes unless Joe confirms. Test each bar: would a mate groan or wince? Anything that does is cut or rewritten plain.
35. **Plain beats clever.** If a bar works because of what is said and how flatly it is said, not because of a pun, keep it (examples that pass: "Booked it for three weeks, then I did four" from the true 21-to-28-day overrun; "Tynwald passed laws; meanwhile, I passed out in the aisle").

## H. Poster test (Joe, 9 Oct 2026: "too specific... a recount of not that interesting stuff I did on holiday... only if they actually sound cool")
36. **Poster test for places and things the narrator visits.** A visited place or object stays only if it would look good in a music video or on a film poster (an Alpine valley in snow, a volcano over ruins, a festival crowd, a ferry at sunset, a mountain pass in fog, a giant bronze spider) or carries status, speed, menace or danger. Itinerary logistics (signage, queues, car parks, fuel stops, hostel breakfasts, honesty boxes, paddock stands, chain-tightening) fail and are cut. This test does NOT apply to the domestic anchors of criterion 16, which are the narrator's voice. 3 = every visited place or object passes; the song never reads as a holiday recount.
37. **Nonchalance first.** [replaces "Swagger first"] The register stays flat, unbothered and understated, in the original's deadpan voice; the narrator never shouts his boasts, he mentions them. 3 = at least 80% of bars are in that voice.

## I. Joe's definition (9 Oct 2026). AUTHORITATIVE.
38. **Wordplay that is clever, carried by rhyme and flow.** A W-bar has a real second meaning found on a second listen (idiom made literal, double meaning, cut-up, homophone, travel word with a crime sense), is neat not groany, and the wordplay lands on or beside the rhyme word, on a beat. Model (Joe): the original's cashing of "lights out" into a type of bulb. Original (Genius pass): W and CW lines are 22 of 81 unique lines (27%); the payoff word sits in the rhyme slot in 10 of 10 CW lines and 8 of 12 W lines; the payoff often lands on the next bar (a 2-3 bar metaphor run cashing one lexical set: bulb, packed, next-day delivery). 3 = at least six such bars, each natural, mostly with the payoff word in the rhyme slot, at least one 2-3 bar run cashing one lexical set, all inside chains of 8 or more.
39. **No gag structure.** No setup-then-punchline jokes built to earn a laugh; the humour is the attitude and the craft. 3 = none present.
40. **Character carried by words alone (audio only).** The persona comes through word choice, register and domestic voice, since there are no visuals. 3 = the narrator is recognisable within the first four bars and consistent throughout.
41. **The rhyme scheme and flow sell it.** Each chain is judged as a unit: the pleasure of the rhyme architecture (soft endings, internal rhymes, vowel carpets, bar-shape variation) is what makes the C-bars and W-bars land. 3 = reading and speaking the chain at tempo is enjoyable even without the content.

## Stop rule
Every graded criterion at 2 or more with none below; two consecutive rounds where the critic finds no weak bars and no hygiene failures; and Joe laughs or smiles at the hook and at least five C-bars and W-bars on his own read-through (his test, not the critics').
