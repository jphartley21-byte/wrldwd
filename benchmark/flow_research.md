# Flow research (14 Oct 2026): how to write lyrics that make the best flows, and what we can measure from text

Eight researchers (academic bar-level, practitioner bar-level, UK/deadpan, song-level and tension, computational, prosody, AI-vocal behaviour, text-metric critique) and one synthesiser; raw output in eval/_flow_research.json.

## What we measured in our own data first (13-14 Oct)
- **The judges' flow score is halo-contaminated.** On 587 scored candidate-instances from 99 judge calls (eval/flow_dataset.jsonl, mined by `python -m ww.eval.mine_judge_data`), text features that track judged quality correlate about equally with sense, character, wordplay and rhyme scores, and the residual flow-specific correlation after removing the call's overall score is about zero (|rho| < 0.07). So a judge-flow score cannot validate a flow feature.
- **Aggregate rhythm statistics are already at parity.** Stress-on-beat, syllables per bar, bar-length variance, stress-pattern recurrence across bars, comma rate: ours v3 is within about 0.4 SD of the original. The gap the judges and Joe hear is not in averages.
- **Where we differ, by the new audit (`python -m ww.cli flow DRAFT`)**: end shape differs from the template on 24% of our bars against 3% of the original's (we write |Sw tails where the template wants |Sww: 0% of our bars end with 2+ trailing weak syllables, 12% of the original's); 8% of our bars contain 4+ unstressed syllables in a row between stresses against 1%; the original ends 14% of its bars mid-clause on a function word so the sentence crosses the bar line, ours 1%; flow flags per bar 1.99 against 1.54 (v1 2.36; the same bars with words shuffled 2.62, so the measure orders the right way).
- **Flow detectors that fire on about half the original's own bars (stress inversion 49%, reduced syllable on a downbeat 51%, stress clash 47%) cannot be hard gates** ('a gate the benchmark fails is a wrong gate'); they are shown as ranking readouts beside each line. Hard gates: syllable count within 1, end shape within a quarter of the bars, no 4-unstressed run beyond one per eight bars, weak syllables on downbeats capped at the original's rate.

## Distilled rules (full list of 24 in eval/_flow_research.json -> synthesis.rules)
- (bar) Process order for every bar: (1) one-line claim, (2) pick the end word and fix it on the template's closing slot, (3) write the bar's strong/weak skeleton from the reference template, (4) back-fill words from the end towards the start, (5) run the counter and the detectors. Never write the sentence first and count afterwards, and never fit the skeleton before the claim exists.
- (bar) Never trust the model's own syllable count. Count externally, repair any bar that is not exactly on the template count (and never over 15), and for each bar produce 8-20 variants, drop the off-count ones, then rerank the rest by the fewest stress mismatches that still read as plain speech.
- (bar) Put the primary-stressed syllable of each content word on a strong template slot and let function words and reduced syllables fill the gaps. Never put the, a, of, to, and or a schwa syllable on a downbeat the reference stresses. Aim at the reference's stress-on-strong rate for that slot, not at 100%.
- (bar) Do not invert a polysyllabic word against the grid: its stressed syllable must be on a slot at least as strong as its unstressed neighbours. If it does not fit, swap the word rather than shifting its stress.
- (bar) Keep 1-2 unstressed syllables between stresses, occasionally 3. Zero only where the template gives each stress its own beat; four or more is rushing. Fix a clash by inserting a short function word or swapping one heavy monosyllable for a word with a lighter shape.
- (bar) The rhyme word is the bar's last stressed word, with at most one weak syllable after it, on the closing strong slot, and on the same slot throughout a rhyme chain. Link rhymes one bar or two beats apart; use a three-beat gap only as a deliberate effect.
- (bar) Use commas in about a quarter of bars (the reference's rate), only after a stressed phrase-edge syllable at the template's pause slot, never between a function word and its content word, and never as a way to patch a bar's count.
- (bar) Build bars from one or two phrases of roughly 5 or 10 syllables; use an odd length (3-4 or 7) only as the variation. Let the template, not the corpus, set the count.
- (bar) Give heavy syllables (long vowel, coda cluster, phrase-final) the slots that are followed by space. Use runs of 3+ sixteenth-spaced syllables only when every syllable in the run is light.
- (bar) The payoff or idiom-cashing word is the bar's last stressed word and the rhyme word. Never place a punchline mid-bar. The setup stays flat and plain, and the payoff may be one word on the closing slot.
- (bar) Write speech rhythm: word order a person would say aloud. After writing, read the bar as a plain sentence and reword if a stranger would not say it so. Never invert syntax or choose odd words only to hit the grid.
- (bar) Get rhyme density from varied internal and frame rhymes (matching consonant skeleton with a changed vowel, assonance on stressed mid-bar syllables), not from repeating a word. Use each end word once per section apart from the chorus return, and save the multisyllabic unit for payoff bars.
- (bar) Displace a stress off the beat only for a deliberate effect, on the payoff bar, by about one syllable, and keep the surrounding bars regular.
- (bar) Put at most one rare place name or foreign word in a bar; if it sits in the rhyme slot it must carry the stress. Test each stubborn token on a 4-line Suno sample and respell it phonetically if it is mangled.
- (song) Plan the flow before any wording: a table with one row per bar giving target syllables, end shape, stress skeleton, group id and role (anchor, transition, breath, punch). Copy it from the reference templates, then fill each cell.
- (song) Hold one rhythmic template for 2-4 bars, then change it, with no run past 4 bars and not a change on every bar. Make the last bar of each group a transitional bar that keeps the old end sound or length and opens in the new shape.
- (song) Plan each section's grouping before writing (8 bars as 4+4 or 2+2+4; 12 as 4+4+4 or 4+4+2+2; 16 as 4+4+4+4), put the largest change at the midpoint, and make the closing bars distinct.
- (song) Give every section one longest and one shortest bar and put the shortest late, carrying the payoff. After a dense run (11+ syllables) drop to a short bar of about 7 or fewer, and make it a breath bar with an empty tail where the template has one.
- (song) Let a sentence cross the bar line only at planned group changes (for example bar 4 to 5, 8 to 9, or into the hook) and mix end-stopped with run-on bars at the reference's measured rate. Keep the hook mostly end-stopped.
- (song) Match the reference's density by section: the hook tighter, the verses looser, with a distinct curve per verse. Raise or lower density by section, not bar by bar, and keep the hook's skeleton identical on every return.
- (song) Cap cadence repetition: no more than three consecutive bars with the same syllable count and end shape. When a callback reuses a bar's rhythm shape it takes new end sounds, and when it reuses an end sound it takes a new shape, never both.
- (song) Benchmark every bar against the reference's own number for that same slot, never against a corpus norm or a generic threshold.
- (song) Suno layout: one lyric line per bar, one clause per line, an end mark on each line, a blank line only between 4-bar blocks or sections, at most one bracketed delivery cue per section, and no instruction text in lyric lines. Put accent, tempo feel and mix in the style field with no fast-tempo words.
- (song) Never use one automatic flow or rhyme score as the objective. Use the detectors to remove obvious failures and to rank, show every fired flag per bar next to the line, and validate any feature on delivered audio (Joe's ear, Suno renders) before it becomes a gate.

## Features proposed (24; status in the system)
Implemented in ww/write/flowaudit.py: syllable-count error, end slot and shape, word-stress inversion, reduced syllable on a strong slot, stress clash, inter-stress gaps, accent-interval histogram, phrase split, enjambment proxy (ends on a function word), adjacent-bar contrast and drops, template runs, cadence monotony, density spread. Not yet: rhyme interval and slot (10), multisyllabic unit placement (12), weight-span fit (19), render-risk lint (21), style-prompt lint (23).

## Contradictions between sources, resolved by the original's own numbers
1. Rhythm-first versus claim-first: practitioner guides and the music-theory material say sketch the slot pattern then fill words; this repo found that fit-first produced noun stacks that say nothing. Resolved in rule 1 as claim, then end word, then skeleton, then fill. 2. Enjambment target: Adams treats enjambment as the sophisticated move (11 of 20 bars end a unit), the researchers' derived targets range from below 0.4 to 30-60% to 40-60%, and a comedy track built on end-of-bar payoffs probably wants mostly end-stopped bars. All of these numbers come from single verses or inference, so the target is the reference's own measured rate. 3. Syllables per bar: MCFlow's 4.5 syl/s gives about 7.7 per bar at 140 BPM, but its totals (62,466 syllables over 5,803 measures) give about 10.8 per measure at slower tempos; Suno guides say 6-12; the reference measures 5-15 (hook mean 8.2, verse 9.4). Take the reference. 4. Stress on strong slots: the literature says 'mostly' (no percentage) and one researcher proposed 0.6-0.8 for hooks, which has no source; the repo's own labelled data show the aggregate percentage ranks approved bars below a rejected one. Only zero-tolerance flags and the phrase-edge version are kept; the aggregate is advisory. 5. Comma use: Hayes-style phrase-edge breaths and the reference's 25% comma rate against Suno folklore that commas add pauses and break flow. Resolved as a rate with placement rules. 6. Rhyme density: MCFlow's about 25% of stressed syllables, Hirjee-Brown's per-artist styles and DopeLearning's generated verses at 21% above the best human (with weaker coherence) disagree on what is 'good'; only the repetition-guarded density relative to the reference is kept. 7. Displaced accents: Adams reads them as tension and a mark of sophistication, Ohriner's music-rhythmic flow sits on the grid, and Skinner-style speech rhythm is behind the beat. No source covers deadpan comic rap, so localisation of displacement to the payoff bar is inference. 8. Short-then-long and build-then-drop shapes are inference from two researchers and are supported only by the reference's own 7-then-13 couplet; Adams's grouping evidence is one verse. 9. Rule: 'avoid triplets / keep duple' is a recommendation from Ohriner's taxonomy, but the text-side bar count of 12 is ambiguous (the reference has 12-13 syllable bars) and triplet versus 16th flow cannot be told apart from counts, so no triplet flag is implemented. 10. Repair loops: Tian et al. show reranking and co

## Traps
Do not hard-code literature numbers (4.5 syllables/s, 25% rhymed, 40-60% enjambment): they are American, mostly full-time, single-verse. The original, slot by slot, is the benchmark. The decisive validation is Joe's ear on Suno renders (A/B), not any judge.
