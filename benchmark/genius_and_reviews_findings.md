# Genius, reviews and production findings (agent report, paraphrased; no lyrics reproduced)

Sources: Genius page (read via Joe's Chrome), reviews and features, WhoSampled, songbpm. Status: Genius, reviews and production are in; YouTube reaction transcripts are still being gathered. Everything below is secondhand and unchecked unless marked.

## Production and credits
- Released 14 Jul 2022 (some sources say 15 Jul). Producers: Handsome Dexter (WhoSampled; Genius also lists "Double C"; AltPress names 91shots and Dexter). Label: Sindhuworld. Writer credit: "Pete & Bas" (authorship is contested in some articles; the duo say they write their own).
- A replayed hook from a Willy William track runs from the very start (WhoSampled). Genius also mentions a second sample. The beat is built around one catchy, instantly recognisable hook riff.
- Tempo: songbpm.com gives 140 BPM (half-time 70), D major, 4/4, 3:01. One source suggests the original sample sits around 105 BPM in B minor and was retimed (the agent's inference). Joe's tap was 142. Joe's pasted AI analysis said G minor with dorian colour, which does not match D major / B minor. Key is therefore unresolved; Suno barely follows keys, so it is a low-stakes disagreement.
- About 96 lyric lines in 3:01 gives roughly one line per bar at 140 BPM (the agent's arithmetic). So lines really are bars, and 12-15 syllable lines are fast bursts.

## Genius annotations (summary by section; 42 unique, mostly unreviewed, three confirmed by the duo)
- **Intro:** a cartoon catchphrase said over the producer's tag, as a joke on the producer's name.
- **Chorus:** the greeting words are real local words for each place (Patois, Yoruba "thanks", French, Italian). "Plainclothes" = undercover police and the pun with "plane" is deliberate. "Leg room" = coming home with more bags. "Transatlantic flavours" = a distribution network. A tonic-wine brand and a double meaning sits in "Magnum". "German whip / stick" = car and gun. "Plates of meat" = rhyming slang for feet.
- **Verse 1:** "handshake / network" = business and hand-to-hand dealing. "Bubbling" = cooking. Ouzo, bald top and curly back are about Bas's own look in the video (self-mockery). Twenty-deck = 20 cigarettes, with the price creeping up 10p a line (inflation joke). Dubai = strict drug laws. "Plod" = police. Slapping a fifty = a bribe. "Drum" = music pun plus a magazine. "Fumez" = a named collaborator. "Spoons" = Wetherspoons. "Duck-duck goose" = children's game as a bat to a debtor.
- **Verse 2:** "Goose" = vodka. Three-piece suit = unlikely-looking rappers. "Red dot" = gun sight. The "U" / "S.A" split hands one word from one rapper to the other, and an annotator calls the rhyme scheme "crazy". "You want some" = a meme. "Dot-dot" = double barrel. "Long johns" = home pyjamas. Barbed-wire bat = a wrestler's famous bat. "Smackdown" = WWE.
- Inferred line attribution (from annotation wording only): the rougher voice does leg room, crack, Dubai, sawn-off and the "U"; the deeper voice does ouzo, haircut, goose game and the "S.A".

## What writers and reviewers say makes it work
- Tight drill production, a catchy sample, raspy voices that suit the beat, energy beyond novelty (Roundhouse Music, Jul 2022).
- They go "back to back" on international moves (GRM Daily premiere). The globe-spanning lyric with Yoruba in it is read as love of the genre (Rolling Stone UK).
- Fans on AOTY praise the beat, the flows, the "flex lyrics", and the producer tag.
- Duo-wide: deadpan delivery of excessive violence, breath control, strong hooks, a deeper voice and a rougher voice, a persona "turned up to eleven", old references mixed with new slang (RapReviews 2021); clichés plus personal detail, self-deprecation and mundane asides, one rapper "linking rhymes bar after bar" (Diamondback 2021); weathered grit, gangster tropes mixed with British references, producer tag a little overused (La Mort de la Musique 2025).

## Extra techniques found (not in my original list)
1. Chains of assonance run across section breaks: the "oo" run holds about 14 of 15 lines from end of verse 1 into verse 2; the "-ee" run about 13 of 16; the "ai" run is carried by lines ending on "I".
2. The title is said once, in a hinge, and the chorus never says it.
3. Places are chosen for their vowel (the chorus places all share the long "ay" sound with plane and flavours).
4. Frame reuse: "just + verb + place" moves the scene (just touched down / just stepped out), and each stop gets one local word or cliché (ouzo, Gibraltar cigarettes, federale, red cup).
5. Reduplicated percussive words (dot-dot, ping-pong, click-clack, duck-duck, chit-chat, naughty-naughty).
6. Idioms made literal (slap on the wrist becomes slapped a fifty; lights out becomes a halogen bulb; shoot that shot is gun and football).
7. More lexical sets: music-as-gun (tune, drum, track, boom) and drinks (goose, ouzo, pint, Spoons, red cup, wine).
8. "then" as a second filler word beside "mate" (come on then, go on then, grab him then).
9. Profanity-free throughout; euphemism carries the menace.
10. Self-mockery inside boasts, and callbacks to their own catalogue.
11. Cross-generation references (a cartoon, wrestling, a 2013 meme, reggae).
12. A producer-tag cold open played as a joke.
