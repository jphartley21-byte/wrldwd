# Technique map of the original (from the lyrics Joe pasted)

Source: the lyrics Joe pasted in chat. Genius annotations were NOT available (Genius is blocked for my fetch tool and Joe declined the browser route), so everything below is read straight from the text. I refer to lines by number (L1 = first line of the pasted text, L76 = last line of the verse before the hook returns) and quote only isolated words.

## 1. Shape of the whole song
- Hook (L1-L16), then a first run in the same "I'm global" voice (L17-L24), then one very long verse (L25-L76), then the hook again (bookend).
- Roughly 60 lines of verse in six long rhyme chains, not couplets and not quatrains.
- Search descriptions say one rapper is gruff and shouty (bravado) and the other links rhymes bar after bar. That fits: L1-L24 reads as the shouted, chantable part, L25 onward as the chained, intricate part. Not confirmed from audio.

## 2. Line lengths (my syllable counts, British pronunciation)
- Average about 9.3 syllables per line; verse average about 9.7. Range 5 to 15.
- 20% of lines are 7 syllables or fewer (L1, L9, L14, L16, L21, L23, L25, L26, L27, L34, L49, L51, L53, L57, L69). These are setups, breaths and punchlines.
- 14% are 12 syllables or more (L13, L24, L28, L29, L31, L41, L42, L43, L48, L66, L70). These are bursts where the rhythm speeds up.
- So a third of the lines are deliberately off-length. The flow is uneven on purpose: short, short, short, then a long run. Example shape: L25 (7), L26 (6), L27 (5), then L28 (12), L29 (12), L30 (10), L31 (12).
- The same stair-step recurs: L49 (6), L50 (9), L51 (7), L52 (10), L53 (5), then L54-L55 back to 9-10.
- Open question: lines of 12-15 syllables will not fit one bar at 142 BPM, so some lines probably run longer than a bar or are delivered in a triplet burst. Joe can settle this by tapping: how many taps from the start of one hook line to the start of the next?

## 4. Internal rhyme and consonance (rhyme inside the bar)
- Two rhymes per bar is the norm in the dense blocks: one mid-bar, one at the end. Examples by line: L29 (Worldwide mid, outside end), L31-L32 (apologise / fraternise mid-bar, time / mine end), L41 (Turkey mid, certi end), L42 (dirty mid, thirsty end).
- Vowel carpets: block C repeats the short "uh" vowel in almost every line (touched, double, bubbling, shovel, smuggling, covered, just). L18 stacks four "uh" sounds in one bar (run, up, numb, mon).
- Mosaic rhymes: matching sounds across different word boundaries. Examples: Dubai / doobies (L48), plain clothes / plane home (L3-L4), lonesome / long one (L70-L71), double it / shovel it.
- Alliteration and anaphora: "German" three times in L13; heavy b-/w-/r- clustering in L74.
- Onomatopoeia as rhythm: chit-chat (L19), click-clack boom (L54), dot-dot (L72), ping-pong (L73).

## 5. Technique catalogue
1. **Roll call with code-switched greetings.** Each place gets a greeting in the local language or slang (Spanish, Jamaican/London, Yoruba, French, Italian). Place names are the rhyme anchors and the structure.
2. **Long monorhyme chains.** 8-16 lines on one sound. Built mostly on weak-syllable endings (-al, -it/-in/-him, -y) so rhymes land on unstressed tails and never sound like nursery couplets.
3. **AXAA break.** One non-rhyming line (L23, L27, L44, L59) sets up the next punchline. It is short and plain, which makes the following line hit harder.
4. **Hinge line.** L53 ends on the next block's sound, so the block change feels like a drop, not a seam.
5. **Cut-up and homophone rhymes.** Phrases that sound alike but split differently (plain/plane, lonesome/long one, apologise/fraternise).
6. **Enjambed ends.** Lines ending on a pronoun (L25, L26) or splitting an acronym across a bar line (L67-L68), so the rhyme lands before the sentence does.
7. **Echo with a twist.** A word is repeated with a changed meaning: slap on the wrist becomes slapped him a fifty (L50-L51); changed his mind becomes changed his tune (L52-L53); smoking (L44) becomes smoking doobies (L48); "goose" as a children's game then as a drink (L60-L61); "laid" used four times (laid a track, laid out in long johns, get laid out); "prego" calls back to "luego".
8. **Number ladder.** thirty, forty, fifty as rhyme words (L45, L46, L51). The same price joke is repeated with one digit changed.
9. **Lexical sets as extended metaphors.** Dealing and violence described in e-commerce and logistics words (ship, packs, postal, cash back, boxed, packaged, shipped, Amazon). The finale describes violence in sports words, one sport per line.
10. **Bathos from British mundanity.** Big boast, then something small and domestic: a pint in Spoons, hobnail boots, twenty-deck prices, a wife bringing cigarettes back from Gibraltar, a bald dome with curly back, "needs must", "naughty, naughty".
11. **Understated violence.** Menace delivered as a children's game (duck-duck goose), a sports result (back of the net) or a tidy parcel (boxed and shipped). The comedy is the calm tone.
12. **Brand and name anchors.** Amazon, Dublin, Fumez (a real collaborator, named mid-song), Spoons, WWE.
13. **Truncation.** "certi" is a cut-off word whose rhyme the ear finishes.
14. **Voice tics as rhythm.** "mate" dropped mid-bar (L50, L54, L55, L60), "come on then", "go on then", "nah", "Lord knows".
15. **Bookend.** The hook returns at the end unchanged.
16. **Escalation then montage.** Boasts get bigger, then slow into leisure (pub, boots), then end on a fast montage of knock-outs.

## 6. What makes it funny (the engine)
Menace and mundanity are set side by side with no wink. Everything is said in the same flat, confident tone, so the listener supplies the joke. The internal rhymes are dense enough that the jokes land on the rhyme word itself (halogen after lights out, Amazon after shipped, fifty after slap).

## 7. Where my v2 draft falls short (honest self-critique)
- Blocks are 4 bars, the original runs 8-16. No sustained chain, so no momentum.
- Every line is similar length (about 9-10 syllables). The original uses short setup lines and 12-15 syllable bursts.
- No AXAA breaks, no hinge, no echo-with-twist, no number ladder, no lexical set doing metaphor work, no cut-up or homophone rhymes, no acronym enjambment.
- Very little internal rhyme. The original usually rhymes twice per bar.
- The hook copies the original's pattern too closely ("greeting to the lads in place"). That template should change.
- Jokes arrive one per block. The original lands one every one or two bars.
- No bookend, no escalation arc, no montage finale.

## 8. Method I'll use for v3 (block-first, not line-first)
1. Pick 6-7 sound families for Joe's material (weak-syllable endings first: -al, -it/-in/-him, -ee, -ow, -ine, -one, -ound).
2. Build a word bank per family from Joe's real life (places, objects, mates, kit, habits), including feminine and cut-up pairs.
3. Write each block of 8-16 lines on one sound with one X line, a hinge into the next block, and two rhymes per bar where possible.
4. Run at least one echo-with-twist, one number ladder, one lexical-set metaphor and one bookend.
5. Vary line length on purpose (about 20% short punch lines, about 15% long bursts).
6. Check each line against the AI-tell list and check that no phrase matches the original.
