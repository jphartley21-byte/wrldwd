Two things the FIRST version of this map got wrong, both fixed here: the end sound used to come
from the final WORD only, so a two-word tail showed as a single vowel; and the scheme came from
the last stressed vowel, so it called the opening AAAA passage `abcc`. The family column is the
one to trust, and the `sch` column is kept beside it so the difference is visible.

Three scheme readings sit side by side, because they genuinely disagree. `scheme_on_tail_syllables`
is the strict measurement. `scheme_human_by_ear` is the annotation made by ear, which is the one
the review checks against. `scheme_loose_by_ear` is a rule tuned to reproduce that annotation: it
reproduces 5 of the 10 slots exactly and agrees on 76% of the pairs overall, so where it differs
from the human column in the verses, one of the two is generous and the difference is worth an
ear. The strict column is what the gates use; the human column is the target.

Under each slot's table there is a **Wordplay** list. It exists because `idiom: yes` in the tags
column was not enough to judge a line by: it said a stock phrase was there without saying which
one, what world it implies, or whether the line uses the phrase WELL. The list names each phrase
and gives the register it signals, and it names any **pun pair** - two words in nearby bars with
identical pronunciation, which is how the original answers a menacing phrase with its mundane
sense a bar later. Bar 3 is the worked example: the phrase for undercover police, a second phrase
signalling caution, the payoff landing **in the rhyme slot**, and the pun pair running into bar 4.

CSV columns: `idioms_named` (phrase plus register), `payoff_in_rhyme_slot` (is the key word the
bar's last stressed syllable - rubric 38), `pun_pair_with` (the partner bar and word).

Three things about wordplay stay HUMAN and are not in this map, because no measure can settle
them: whether the phrase is meant literally or figuratively, whether the cashed reading actually
lands as a joke, and whether it reads as deadpan character rather than a pun that announces
itself. Those are gates 12, 13, 37 and 38 in benchmark/gate_coverage.md.

`n/a` in the beats column means the alignment found no vocal in that bar. A negative audio step is
a pickup before the downbeat. The CSV's `bars_spanned` column says how many audio bars a phrase
covers when it runs past one. Bars 1-8 are the same audio as 77-84 (the chorus repeat).
