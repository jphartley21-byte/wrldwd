# ww verses v2 pilot (13 Oct 2026): method v2 on the three weakest sections

Method v2 = plan + critic, concept mining (30 concepts, best 4 picked), writers under hard checker gates (narrator in the bars, end-rhyme carpet, no repeated end words, declared cashed idioms with the payoff word last), screen, two repair rounds accepted only if two judges prefer the challenger, final blind score. Judges read the original; the v27 control was hidden in every pool.

**Reading it honestly.** Two of three sections improved against the round-1 block scored by the same judge (V1 25-32 +2.5, Hook 9-16 +1.5); V1 33-44 got worse (-1.5). Mean final score 5.3 against 5.0 for round 1, original 8.5-9: a small gain that sits inside the judges' noise. The same block was scored 7.0 and 4.25 by different judge pairs (V1 33-44), so absolute scores are unreliable and in-loop acceptance at a 0.25 margin was too loose. What clearly moved: the checkable properties (narrator in the bars, no repeated end words, carpet, declared cashes) and the judges' character score (7 in two sections, was 4-6). What did not move: flow (3-5) and rhyme quality (4.5-5), and forced puns that announce themselves ('cook the books' -> a stew) still cost sense.

The v2.1 script (ww_sections_v2 with a three-judge ensemble, stricter acceptance, a pun-stew filter on concepts and a per-bar claim audit) is ready but not run.

    Slowest bit, then it's the forms I'm signing
    Car in the harbour? Ticked tidal
    Owner? I wrote the name of a rival

Cashed idioms declared: bar 6 'cook the books' -> stew; bar 10 'jumped the queue' -> queue; bar 12 'black and blue' -> blue

Invented / persona to confirm: Hugh (Keith's forged signature name), the rival whose name is put as owner, Bristol custody desk and 'Sarge', the council's bin permit and thank-you letter, Dave's neck/Thursday tap (persona), Keith as signwriter (persona), the auditor visit and beef shin stew, Mum and the bowl of stew. Gnome, Boomtown/Ahoy wristbands and Castletown harbour are real; the car in the harbour is invented.

Final judge, abstract gaps: The reference holds one narrator who reports something illicit or boastful in a flat everyday register, makes every bar a single followable claim, and lands a cashed phrase or a perfect rhyme on the last word. The candidates have the register at best intermittently: they drift in and out of nonsense, lean on filler tails and repeated rhyme words, and either cash idioms into pun-stews (C1) or barely cash any (C3). Rhyme families are held only in the back half, there is little internal or multisyllabic rhyme, and bar lengths are uniformly short with no run-on or deliberate long bar, so the flow lacks the reference's rhythm contrasts. The strongest candidate (C2) is closest because it tells a small story with a recurring victim, but still falls well short of parity.

Final judge, abstract gaps: The best candidate has the right kind of character, a deadpan domestic narrator reporting petty crime in an everyday register, but it lacks the reference's travel-and-place texture, any holiday-abroad stakes, and a held rhyme carpet with a soft multisyllabic tail. Its idiom payoffs are not consistently in the rhyme slot. The reference is built on a single fixed vowel family with strong internal rhyme, a price gag, a concrete foreign setting and a throwaway closer. No candidate has all four. The middle candidate's strong three-bar rhyme set collapses, and the weakest is mostly noun-stack nonsense with a repeated end word. Bar lengths in all three are uneven in a way that reads as accident, not as deliberate short-then-long variation.

## Hook 9-16

    I write Dave's name in the red
    Owes me twelve grand and a loaf of bread
    Boomtown at night, with Dave starring
    Heads will roll, I said. Dave lost his head
    It's a prop, so I sharpened the blade in the shed
    Rolled ten feet. Not far
    I told Keith keep an eye on the rest
    Keith keeps the eye in a jar

Cashed idioms declared: bar 1 'in the red' -> red; bar 4 'heads will roll' -> head; bar 8 'keep an eye on' -> jar

Invented / persona to confirm: Dave (cast, debtor, per plan); Keith (cast, per plan); 'twelve grand and a loaf of bread' (invented amount, persona); Dave starring at the Boomtown guillotine, the sharpened blade and the eye in a jar are invented cartoon flourishes on the true Boomtown wooden guillotine prop (the prop and Boomtown are real, the rest is persona). 'Rolled ten feet' is an invented figure.

Final judge, abstract gaps: The reference keeps one vowel family running through all eight bars, so the carpet is the joke engine. Every bar is a short, confident, boastful claim made in a swaggering voice and landing on a place or loaded word in the rhyme slot. Even its nonsense-adjacent moments are scannable at pace. The candidates fall short in three ways. Their rhyme is split across two or three families with unpaired bars, so there is no carpet. Their bars are sentence-like and unevenly long, with full stops mid-line and filler setups, so they do not run at pace. Their payoffs are announced puns rather than quietly cashed idioms. The best candidate has a distinctive deadpan voice and a literalised idiom or two, but it lacks the reference's unbroken sound pattern, its bar-to-bar momentum and its world of named places and local words, and it undercuts its own deadpan by explaining a bar. The weakest candidates are word-lists with no narrator and no claims.
