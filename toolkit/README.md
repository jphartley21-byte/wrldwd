# toolkit: rhyme and flow checks (offline, no model tokens)

Needs the `cmudict` pip package (installed). US CMU pronunciations with a UK-ish layer (post-vocalic R dropped for rhyme matching). Out-of-dictionary words (place names, slang) go in the OVR table at the top of rhyme_tools.py as ARPAbet; the tool prints "OOV word" when it hits one.

```
python toolkit/rhyme_tools.py pron "double it"                 # phones, UK-ish phones, syllables
python toolkit/rhyme_tools.py rhymes tobago --n 0               # n=0: from the last stressed vowel (default). n=2,3: last N syllables
python toolkit/rhyme_tools.py rhymes "double it" --n 3 --mode assonance --max 40   # vowel pattern only (dactylic carpets)
python toolkit/rhyme_tools.py mosaic "double it" --nsyl 3       # cut-up rhymes: word pairs that sound like the phrase; --loose merges voiced/unvoiced
python toolkit/rhyme_tools.py families --min 3 --top 25         # rhyme families ranked by how many words from theme_words.txt they hold
python toolkit/rhyme_tools.py syllmap chain.txt     # every syllable mapped; perfect/slant/assonance, multisyllabic runs, 2-syl end tails, alliteration
python toolkit/rhyme_tools.py check chain.txt                   # one bar per line, blank line = new chain; "-" reads stdin
```

`check` prints per bar: syllables, accent upper bound (monosyllables count as stressed except function words, so treat as a ceiling), end word, rhyme key from the last stressed vowel, vowel carpet, same-vowel word pairs inside the bar, flags (LEN <5 or >14, STRESS <3 or >8, OOV, banned words), plus per chain: vowel-carpet counts, adjacent end-sound match depth in phones, internal-rhyme bar count, repeated end words, "mate" count.

Benchmark numbers from the original's opening 8 bars: 5, 9, 9, 9, 8, 10, 8, 8 syllables; carpet EY x5, OW x2, EH x1; one perfect rhyme (favours/flavours); sparse internal rhyme.

Edit theme_words.txt to steer `families` toward the vocabulary you want (categories are # comment lines; use _ for spaces in phrases).
- `python toolkit/flow_check.py -`: per-bar stress clashes, weak runs, mid-bar pauses (commas), consonant pile-ups and mouth-shape transitions; put the original bars (pasted, not saved) above a `---` line to compare. A text screen, not an ear.
  Mouth-shape transitions (`joins()` / `rare_joins()`, 14 Oct 2026) name where the mouth must reorganise across a word boundary: a fricative into /h/ (glottal reset), a voiced fricative into a liquid, or a same-place stop into a stop. The blunt 3+ consonant count is NOT the signal (the original averages 0.93 of those per bar); the rare set is, and the original's par -- `--calibrate PATH`, counts only -- is 0 in 78% of its 92 bars and 2+ in 6. Two of the three types occur 3 times each in the whole song, and Joe's ear found the bar that contains two of them before any tool could (the word involved, "he's", had no pronunciation at all: see `pron()` in rhyme_tools.py). `--selftest` asserts the measure fires on that bar and stays quiet elsewhere.
- `python toolkit/helpers.py freq|uk|tails|rp|dm` (see benchmark/flow_references.md): British IPA, rare-word flags, RhymePad scheme/groups/meter, Datamuse rhyme lists.
- `python toolkit/match.py --slot "V1 1-8" -`: hard PASS/FAIL per bar against the original profile (numbers only, benchmark/original_profile.json).
- `python toolkit/flow_fit.py START_BAR -`: our bars vs the original rhythm measured from audio (benchmark/flow_template.json, built by flow_map.py in the "flow" conda env).
- `python toolkit/phon.py "a" "b"`: graded British rhyme score (perfect/near/slant). match.py now includes a rhyme-strength map vs the original.
- `python toolkit/trybars.py --slot "Hook 9-16" cands.txt|-[--detail]`: the FAST inner loop. Scores any number of candidate blocks (blank-line separated; `#` comments ok) in ONE call and ranks them by (form problems, judge risk). Reports per bar: syllables vs the slot's frame, tail vowels, the by-ear rhyme families it actually forms, whether the ending is hard/soft/tagged, meaning and coherence fails, the full check_all problem list, and the judge's per-bar risk. `--detail` adds the features pushing the weakest bar toward reject. check_all costs 264 ms/bar (it spawns three subprocesses) against ~2 ms/bar for everything else, so its results are memoised to the temp dir: ~3 s per new block cold, ~0.5 s warm. Use it instead of running check_all, the judge and the attribution by hand. `--selftest` must pass (asserted by calibrate.py).
- `python toolkit/carpet.py --slot "Hook 9-16"`: the PHASE-A end-word tool. Pick the SOUND before writing the words. It reads the slot's measured target from `benchmark/original_profile.json` (syllables per bar, the scheme, the measured carpet) and `benchmark/flow_template.json` (the per-bar end SHAPE measured from the audio), searches `material/*.md` (true places, with the file that documents each), `toolkit/theme_words.txt`, `toolkit/local_words.txt`, `toolkit/entities.txt` and the OVR table, and proposes (a) a per-bar BANK of end words that fit that bar's vowel, shape and length, commonest first, with places starred, and (b) whole-slot SETS where every pair the by-ear scheme requires clears `match.best_end >= --threshold` (default 0.78; aim 0.95) and at least `--min-places` words are documented places. A complete carpet (the hook quads, V2 1-8) is searched as ONE clique; a looser slot is grouped by (scheme letter, end vowel, end shape), and a group with no clique is printed as UNMET rather than hidden - demanding a rhyme across two different end vowels fails a benchmark the original itself fails. Caches to `benchmark/carpets_<slot>.json`. `--selftest` must pass (asserted by calibrate.py). This replaces "ask a model for rhyme words", which is what produced every rejected block.
- `python toolkit/bank.py <cmd>`: the WRITER'S SUPPLY. `slot --ends "ɑː ɑː eɪ ɑː aɪ eɪ əʊ eɪ"` prints, per bar, the end sound the frame needs and the words + cashed phrases that fit it; `phrases ɑː [--sense money]` lists stock phrases whose payoff word ends on that sound; `words eɪ --syl 1` lists common words by end sound (British vowels, via craft_scan, with wordfreq junk filtered); `slang [DOMAIN] [--end ɒ] [--rare]` lists the harvested slang LAYERS by domain — `money drugs weapons firearms crime prison violence vulgar swear slang british informal` — because the ledger tags each word with the Wiktionary category it came from; `check` reports phrases whose payoff has no pronunciation; `uk` prints the everyday British words wordfreq under-rates. Phrases live in `toolkit/phrases.txt` (225 curated, `phrase | payoff word | sense`) and are the same list `coherence_gate.py` uses for the cashed-idiom check, so what a writer picks from is what the scoring recognises. Three more banks sit behind it: `toolkit/phrases_raw.txt` (6,980 harvested stock phrases, ranked by how common their words are), `toolkit/entities.txt` (5,333 names — 1,446 given names, 717 surnames, 527 cities, 520 people (rappers, comedians, YouTubers), 487 TV, 440 bands, 260 brands, 209 countries, 204 UK towns, 203 game series, 164 memes, 156 films), and `toolkit/vocab_ledger.jsonl` (4,516 words, each tagged with the categories it came from — English slang 1,003, vulgarities 686, firearms 270, weapons 242, crime 239, violence 186, money 104, recreational drugs 105, prison 94, prostitution 92, swear words 53; roughly half of everything fetched is dropped as structural junk, i.e. no dictionary pronunciation and no commonness).
 Query names with `bank.py entities [--type film|game|brand|meme|person] [--end əʊ] [--types]`.- SWEARING AND VULGAR SLANG ARE IN THE POOL AND ARE NEVER DISCOURAGED (Joe, 13 Oct 2026). `harvest.VULGAR` still exists but is only a LABEL: it no longer drops a phrase, a word or a name, which is what it used to do (bitch and dick were missing from it, so 23 and 8 entries walked through while shit and fuck were silently deleted). No gate rejects a swear — verified by running the meaning and regression gates on a block containing one. The writer prompts say swearing is allowed and never softened.
- `python toolkit/characters.py FILE` — Joe's CHARACTER RULE (13 Oct 2026: "limit any reference to a character to 2 references; prefer romantic partners or mates over family"). - `python toolkit/devices.py FILE|-`: the FIGURES OF SPEECH, by name. Simile (`like`/`as` frames, split into plain and CLICHE), metaphor candidates (a vehicle noun predicated of something, or subject and predicate in different lexical fields - the theme-word sections are the fields), bathos (a bland setup clause then a short deflating one landing on something mundane), litotes (negation + downgrader), hedge (modal/softener), hyperbole (absolute quantifiers), anaphora (three or more bars sharing an opener, or the same opener twice inside one bar) and polysyndeton (and-chains). `--calibrate ORIGINAL_TXT` prints counts only for the original, never the lyrics. `--selftest` must pass: it fires on the labelled fixtures in `toolkit/tests/devices_positive.txt`, stays quiet on plain reportage there AND on the Joe-rejected stanzas in `toolkit/tests/`. Reported per block by `trybars.py` as a `figures of speech` line, and carried in the feature dict (not in the judge's vector).
- `python toolkit/check_all.py --slot "V1 17-24" -` or `--song FILE`: EVERY automated check in one verdict. Commands: /review (check bars), /write (parallel agents loop until checks pass). Original lyrics: C:\Users\Joe\Documents\Worldwide_private\original_lines.txt (outside the project).
- `python toolkit/rubric_score.py FILE|-`: scores each 4-bar quad on the 13 MEASURABLE rubric criteria (benchmark/benchmark_rubric.md) with the original's measured numbers as par (`benchmark/original_profile.json`), printing BEATS/MATCH/BELOW per criterion and the below-par list. The other 28 criteria are printed as MANUAL (chain-level or human). Below par means the original does more of that mechanical thing; it does not mean the bar is bad.
  - `--literary`: adds the human layer â€” proxy signals (full-clause bars, theme sets touched, repeated words) plus a 0-3 CRITIC SHEET for the 28 human criteria, so literary quality is scored systematically, not by vibe.
  - `--blocks ORIG_LINES_TXT`: scores the ORIGINAL's own blocks (reads the private file, prints numbers only) â€” the per-block targets.
  - `--vs ORIG_LINES_TXT FILE|-`: per quad, prints "beats on N / loses on M -> BEATS/MATCH/loses" against EVERY original block, so one quad can be matched to the block it beats.
  - `--slot "V1 1-8" FILE|-`: score ONE block against ONE slot's own profile, compared PER BAR, printing the verdict and the criteria it loses on. This is the fair "does this block beat the original's block for this slot" test; use it before any other comparison.
- `python toolkit/meaning_gate.py FILE|-`: the MEANING half of the review, mechanised (gates G1/G3). Flags bars whose clause runs off the end (a determiner, preposition, conjunction or bare auxiliary as the end word), bars with no content words at all, filler frames ("and I am", "and it is"), template openers, redundant pronoun enjambment, too many reduplications, repeated 3-grams, and two places in consecutive verse bars. Run it BEFORE check_all: check_all measures form, this measures whether the bar says anything. `--slot` adds the place-density check; `--selftest` must pass (it is asserted by calibrate.py).

  Library use (imported by the lineup scripts): `block_stats(bars)`, `per_bar(stats, nbars)`,
  `rate_margin(bars, slot_par)` and `rate_verdict(bars, slot_par)`. Rate the comparison PER BAR
  whenever the block is shorter than the slot profile: a 4-bar quad must never be asked to match an
  8-bar slot's raw counts. `rate_margin` is the fair "does this quad beat this slot" number, and it
  is what a lineup should select on; raw counts may only be compared at equal bar counts.
- `python toolkit/research.py`: the four lookups a bar needs, so research is the default move rather than a last resort. `word W` (cmudict + britfone + our overrides + syllable pattern + whether the original uses it), `rhymes W` / `near W` / `sounds W` (local dictionary **and** Datamuse online), `mine QUERY` (greps Joe's own material: travel history, the photo/video-derived place specifics, the place sound families), `slang W` (Wiktionary). Everything it returns is a CANDIDATE: facts still come from `material/` or get tagged `(invented)`/`(persona)`, and every line still has to pass the gates. It now COMPOUNDS: every lookup is written to the vocab ledger, online results are cached (a repeat query costs no network call), and candidates are banked. `--refresh` forces a fresh call, `--dry` records nothing. Only a headword of <= 3 words ever leaves the machine (a bar is >= 4 words, so a lyric line cannot be sent even by accident); `mine` is entirely local.
- `python toolkit/forge.py`: **exhaustive search over everything already written.** It mines every bar and every assembled block in `writing/*.md` (tab-row quads included), enumerates every combination that satisfies the slot's measured rhyme scheme, hard-filters with the meaning gate and the scheme, then ranks survivors by an objective where the human-gate judge is weighted highest - because QUAD A passed every measured gate and was rejected. The top of the ranking is the answer; no human input is needed until the end. `--slot "Hook 5-8" --topk 12 --cap 14`; writes `writing/forge_<slot>.md`. `--selftest` must REDISCOVER Joe's locked bars, which is how the objective is kept honest.
- `python toolkit/pipeline.py`: the continuous version. It loops the forge over all 9 open slots (Hook 1-4 is locked and never targeted), pools only NEW quads the judge does not flag (`risk < 0.45`), and widens its search budget each cycle. The pool it writes lives in `writing/`, so the NEXT cycle mines it and the corpus feeds itself. Artifacts: `writing/pipeline_pool.md` (the growing pool), `pipeline_results.jsonl` (one JSON row per slot per cycle), `pipeline_log.md` (heartbeat), `pipeline_status.json`. `--once` / `--cycles N`; stop it by creating `writing/PIPELINE_STOP`.
- `python toolkit/vocab.py`: the persistent vocabulary ledger (`toolkit/vocab_ledger.jsonl`), one line per word with British IPA, syllable pattern, end-sound tail, rhyme family, whether the original's lines use it, and a **G2 recognisability verdict**. `info WORD` (computed fresh), `g2 WORD|-` (verdict for end words), `show [--rare|--family KEY|--all]`, `selftest` (asserted by calibrate.py). G2 is a PROXY, not a gate: it uses `wordfreq` Zipf < 3.0 (the same threshold as `helpers.py freq`), and `wordfreq` is US-weighted, so it under-scores real British words ("skint", "winnebago") - the verdict prompts Joe's ear and never vetoes a word on its own. Structural junk is dropped when banking bulk candidates, but an explicit `word W` lookup is always kept.
- `python toolkit/sense_checks.py FILE`: the two checks Joe forced after a quad passed the gates but sounded worse - `list_fragments` (bars that chain and/or between phrases with no finite verb: a list, not a thought) and `hops` (consecutive bars with no link at all). `forge.py` also compares **lexical field spread** and **stressed-vowel drift pairs** against the original's own block, so a quad that rhymes only on an unstressed vowel, or opens more scenes than the original does there, FAILS.
- `python toolkit/make_original_map.py`: regenerates benchmark/original_map.md and original_map.csv, ONE readable/editable map of the original (flow measured from the audio, by-ear rhyme scheme, per-line technique tags, chains, device inventory). Generated from the measured JSON, so it cannot drift, and it writes line numbers and isolated words only (rule 4). Each slot also gets a **Wordplay** block: every stock phrase NAMED with the register it signals, whether the payoff lands in the rhyme slot (`payoff_in_rhyme_slot`), and any cross-bar **pun pair** (`pun_pair_with`, words in nearby bars with identical pronunciation - `idioms_in`, `homophone_pairs` and `payoff_in_rhyme_slot` in `craft_scan.py`). Whether a phrase is literal or figurative, and whether the cashed reading lands, stay HUMAN gates 12/13/37/38.

- `python toolkit/phrase_audit.py FILE|-`: the SONG-LEVEL repetition guard. A line repeated verbatim, a content opener used more than 3x, a content 4-gram used more than twice, or commas on more than 30% of bars. Built 13 Oct 2026 after the assembled song came out with 8 bars opening "Might", 7 opening "Booked", 16 leaning on "and the whole / the whole of the" and one line repeated word for word -- every frame legal to a section-level check, and invisible because the song had never been assembled before. Thresholds are CALIBRATED, not guessed: `--calibrate ORIGINAL_TXT` prints counts only and the original PASSES all four (its chorus returns bar for bar -- exempt by design -- and it opens 12 bars with "I"; 23 of its 92 bars carry a comma, so commas are a RATE, not a ban). `--selftest` proves it still catches a duplicate, a tic opener, a repeated phrase and a comma flood. Wired into calibrate.py.
- `python toolkit/recount.py FILE|--slot NAME -`: CLAIMS vs DIARY (14 Oct 2026). Per bar (spaCy, deterministic): a past-tense event with no stance (no modal, imperative or present-tense claim by the speaker), or an itinerary bar (stop one / then PLACE / PLACE next / off to PLACE). Per section: recount share, the longest run of recounted bars, itinerary bars; per song: the recount share. Par is the ORIGINAL's own (`--calibrate ORIGINAL_TXT`, counts only): no recount bars in a hook, at most 3 in a row in a verse, no itinerary, 20% of the song. `--negatives` asserts the three named recounts fail (the v17 itinerary, the v17 Hanoi diary, the v5 jeep). Wired into calibrate.py, songcheck.py and ww slotcheck (HARD; WW_RECOUNT=0 switches it off). Limit: it reads TENSE, so a story retold in the historic present passes it.

Candidates "N | text" against bar N's audio frame (flow_fit), then a whole draft: frame, by-ear scheme pairs (rapfit; the old grader is a note), rapfit's block rules and recount. Promoted from the session scripts that took the v5 redraft to 74/74. Since 15 Oct 2026 `song` also runs ww/write/slotcheck per section (the gate) and reads `#cash` lines; bar 10 is held to the profile's 9 syllables, not treated as free.
- `python toolkit/swagger.py FILE`: BOAST vs SHRUG (15 Oct 2026, after Joe: "reads like a diary not a rap song"). Song level: hook STAMPS (bars with a greeting, local word or place; the original 9/16) and verse SWAGGER (bars with an illicit/menace field, vice, a line at 'you', a warning, or a first-person superlative claim; the original 25/60). `--calibrate ORIGINAL_TXT` (counts only) asserts the original passes; `--negatives` asserts v5, v6 and the three 14 Oct premise hooks fail. A lexicon proxy: it says whether the song boasts, not whether the boast is good. Wired into calibrate.py and songcheck.
