# Rap premises (15 Oct 2026)

Joe on the 14 Oct premises: "This still reads like a diary not a rap song. The premises ... should be like actual rap song premises."

He was right. "Shoestring", "missed it" and "errand" were comedy character studies: every one deflated, against the song bible's
revision 2 ("swagger first; the boast leads; mundane comedowns about one bar in eight"). A rap song's premise is a CLAIM OF STATUS.
Here the joke is the original's: full swagger, small or petty evidence, said deadpan.

The bank lives in `ww/lex/banks/rap_archetypes.json`. Each archetype names real rap songs built on the same premise (title and artist
only, never lyrics), the moves it runs on, the inversion, and the ingredients (material/ingredients.md) that feed it.

| archetype | the claim | reference songs | the inversion (the joke) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Most Wanted** | I'm wanted all over the world | Ice Cube, AmeriKKKa's Most Wanted; Giggs, Talkin' da Hardest | the rap sheet is real and petty: a WANTED FOR EXCESSIVE ENERGY poster, a gnome off a lorry, jeans past a bouncer, a bike in a hedge |
| **Empire** | my empire runs on every continent | Rick Ross, Hustlin'; Nas, The World Is Yours | the rail pass is the jet, the hostel the safe house, the panniers the stash, the gnome his statue |

| Passport | my passport's a trophy cabinet | Pitbull, International Love; Curren$y, Jet Life | every stamp a flex, every currency small change |
| Started From Reading | from nothing to international | Drake, Started from the Bottom; Biggie, Juicy | the riches are a million dong and a pink top hat |
| Every City's a Rave | wherever I land it's a party | DJ Snake & Lil Jon, Turn Down for What; Dizzee Rascal, Bonkers | the parties are King's Day, a wet Boomtown, a Wasteland door |

The first three are built to full hooks (`writing/premises/hook_most_wanted.md`, `hook_empire.md`, `hook_know_a_guy.md`); Most Wanted,
the one with the most true material, is built to a full song. The other three stay as pitches.

**Rap moves** (the bar jobs, replacing claim/flex/deflate/aside):
- brag
- flex list
- shout-out: a local greeting and a place, never the original's frame
- threat: flat, cartoon
- name-drop
- hater: a line at "you"
- punchline: a stock phrase cashed in the rhyme slot
- title drop: once, in a verse
- callback
- deflate: at most one bar in eight

**Measured.** `toolkit/swagger.py` counts hook stamps and verse swagger. The original's hook has 9 of 16 and its verses 25 of 60. v5, v6 and the 14 Oct premise hooks all fail it.
