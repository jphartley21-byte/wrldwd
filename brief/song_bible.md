# Song bible (working title: "Walk In Each")

## Premise and voice
- A deadpan, gruff British rapper boasting about a world tour in the register of a crime boss, but the empire is a rail pass, a hostel bunk, a motorbike with panniers, a rucksack, a festival wristband. The boast is huge, the comedown is small and British. The humour lives in the rhyme words and the flat delivery, not in anecdotes (Joe: "the original isn't a list of real funny stories; I worry it'll become cringe").
- Allowed: deadpan drug references and cartoonish menace, never instructional or preachy. No swearing. No hobby-nerd material. No artist, film or brand names (descriptions instead). True or flagged "invented".
- Delivery: flat and gruff; a second deeper voice takes the handoffs (a split word across the bar line) and some blocks.

## Tempo and bar budget (142 BPM, one line per bar, mean 9.3 syllables, 20% lines 7 or fewer, 14% 12 or more)
Intro 2 | Hook 8 | Verse 1: Block A 10 + Block B (set-piece) 10 + Block C 4 | Hook 8 | Verse 2: Block D 10 + Block E 10 + Block F 4 | Breakdown 8 (handoff) | Verse 3: Block G 16 | Final hook 8 | Outro 2. About 100 bars, about 2:50. Cut to about 80 lines for the first Suno test if needed.

## Hook (8 bars): roll call chosen by vowel
Four couplets, each pairing two real places that end on the same sound, each line = one accurate local word + one concrete weird visual (from photos) + the place or a rhyme word. Never says the title. Never uses the '[greeting] to the boys in [place]' frame. Return unchanged at the end.
- Couplet 1 (-am): Vietnam / Amsterdam. Couplet 2 (-ao): Koh Tao / Bilbao. Couplet 3 (-ay): Byron Bay / Zell am See ('zell am zay'). Couplet 4 (-ana/-ava): Ljubljana / Bratislava.
- Weird visuals available: Bilbao's giant bronze spider; Zell am See's duck dipping its head; Ljubljana's pink church and tram wires; Amsterdam all in orange; scooters in Vietnam; a ferry; a dorm bunk.
- Tag after the hook (spoken, handed to the second voice): the plan, "nine countries, a walk in each".

## Blocks (sound, device, true material)
- **Block A, Interrail (10 bars), chain on -ana/-ava/-enna (Ljubljana, Bratislava, Vienna, banana, bandana, lava, antenna).** Number ladder from the real plan: 21 days, at least 9 countries, a walk in each; reality about four weeks, 7 countries, 4 walks in the photos. One short plain X line before the punchline. Hinge: last bar ends on a word with the Block B vowel (-ile). True material: Lauterbrunnen's 72 waterfalls with one thin visible one and knee-deep snow; Florence (David, the Galileo museum, Fiesole's terracotta roofs); Ljubljana's pink church; Vienna (the Freud museum, cathedral in scaffolding); Zell am See's cycle path to snow peaks; Munich's Bayern match; Black Forest honesty drinks stalls with a cash box.
- **Block B, signature set-piece (10 bars, double-time 11-14 syllables), single sound -ile (mile, Isle, while, aisle, meanwhile, worthwhile, turnstile, profile, denial, style, trial, tile, smile).** Joins the Isle of Man ride (2023) and the Amsterdam cinema aisle (King's Day 2019) through the homophone isle/aisle/I'll, two or three rhymes per bar, every bar a pun, ending on a short plain punchline. The most quotable moment of the song.
- **Block C, 4 bars, X line plus punchline, hinge into the hook.**
- **Block D, Australia 2017 and South-East Asia 2015 (10 bars), chain on -ay/-ains or -ans (Byron Bay, Hervey Bay, plans, vans, sands, Cairns).** Lexical set: hostel and travel admin as crime words (hostel = safe house, dorm = holding cell, visa = licence, passport = alibi). An echo-with-a-twist. True material: seven weeks on the east coast, a 4WD beach, Whitsundays, Magnetic Island, Cairns.
- **Block E, motorbike tours (10 bars), chain on -oats/-all/-ao (John o' Groats, Donegal, Cornwall, boats, coats, wall, fall).** Lexical set: bike and camping gear as crime gear (panniers as stash, chain, paddock stand, passing place, ferry deck as a border run). True material: Isle of Man in sea fog, Donegal's map-reading clifftop, Larrau's cows in fog, the chain tightened in a Luxey garage, Porto's rabelo boats, the ferry home, Dunnet Head's most-northerly stone, Laruns' sheep flood.
- **Block F, festivals (4 bars):** Boomtown wet 2019 to dry 2025, Rolling Loud Rotterdam without naming artists, Wasteland by euphemism, the Rotterdam gnome by euphemism. Understated, not explained.
- **Breakdown (8 bars, handoff):** a place name split across the bar line and finished by the second voice (Bar- / celona; Mar- / rakesh; Pom- / peii), Marrakesh's orange pyramids and Pompeii's Vesuvius, ending on the short bar.
- **Block G, Verse 3 (16 bars): the Three Peaks ladder (Ben Nevis, Scafell Pike, Snowdon), the 72-waterfalls counting joke if unused, Norway's snowy walks, Black Forest honesty stalls, a final deadpan comedown (rain, a flask, the drive home).**

## Techniques that must appear (from the benchmark)
Long chains on soft endings; X lines; hinge lines; two rhymes per bar; cut-up/mosaic rhymes; enjambed split words; reduplicated percussive words (click-clack type); idioms made literal; echo-with-a-twist; a number ladder; two lexical sets (travel admin as crime; bike and camping gear as crime gear); British bathos; euphemism for menace and drugs; voice tics ('mate', 'then'); a bookend hook; escalation to a fast montage; title held back (said once in a hinge, never in the hook).
