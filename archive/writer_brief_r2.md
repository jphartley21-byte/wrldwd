# Writer brief, round 2 (revision 2, 9 Oct 2026, after Joe's steer and the Genius line-by-line pass). Read all of it, then write.

## What this is
A rap for Joe (33, from Reading, lives in Bristol, strict vegetarian) in the style of "Mr Worldwide" by Pete & Bas: a deadpan British world-tour song at 142 BPM, gruff flat voice. Audience: Joe and his mates. It is AUDIO ONLY, so the character has to come through the words and the rhythm alone. Earlier bars were judged "lame, cringe, don't flow" and "miles off". Everything so far is raw. Write more than you need, then cut hard.

## THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND
Joe, who knows the original best, says: there are no jokes in it. The comedy comes from CHARACTER and WORDPLAY, and the RHYME SCHEME and FLOW sell both. A line-by-line study of the original (42 annotations, about 81 distinct lines) agrees. Do not write gags, setup-and-punchline, stand-up lines, or puns that announce themselves.

CHARACTER (nonchalance). The nonchalant lines narrate something extraordinary (violence, bribery, smuggling, being away somewhere shady) in an EVERYDAY BRITISH ADMIN OR CONSUMER REGISTER: a parcel delivery, a pub chain, a schoolboy telling-off, cigarette prices, a wife's duty-free. They usually come as a RUN OF 2-4 BARS: a bland, plain setup bar; then the flat report; then a small domestic closer landing on the rhyme word. The narrator never reacts to danger; violence is euphemised as logistics or games. A domestic person (a wife, Mum) is MENTIONED, not spoken to: the narrator talks to the audience or his mates. Example of the effect (do NOT reuse the frame): in the original he is away in a rich desert city smoking weed and passes word to his wife, via a mate, that he'll be back shortly. It is funny because he is so relaxed: the size of the thing against the smallness of how it's said. The line just ends. Nobody winks.

WORDPLAY. A stock phrase is cashed into a literal thing: the original turns "lights out" into a type of bulb, a "slap on the wrist" into a banknote, "tune" and "drum" into a gun. The cashed-in word sits IN THE RHYME SLOT (the last word of the bar), and the payoff often lands on the NEXT bar, so a 2-3 bar run can cash one set of words (a bulb, then packed, then next-day delivery). It works because the rhyme and flow carry it. Not groaner puns.

A third of the original is simply ACCURATE REFERENCES (places, local words, brands). A correct local word counts for a lot.

OUR persona: a 33-year-old from Reading who lives in Bristol, travelling the world completely nonchalantly. Two directions both work: (a) true travel feats and festival strangeness reported flatly in everyday British register; (b) travel words with a crime second meaning (connections, booked, held up, flight risk, travel light). Cartoonish invented crime-boss flourishes on true places are allowed only if you tag them (invented).

## TWO BAR TYPES (every bar is C, W, or CW)
C-bar test (NONCHALANCE): 1) the thing is big, illicit, dangerous or absurd; 2) it is reported flat, in an everyday British register; 3) no punchline, no wink, no explaining; 4) concrete, I can see it.
W-bar test (REWIND): 1) a real second meaning, found on a second listen; 2) neat, not a groan; 3) the payoff word is the LAST word and a satisfying rhyme (soft or multisyllabic where possible); 4) it scans so the beat carries it.

## Frames to AVOID (they are the original's templates)
"tell her I'm out in [place] doing [thing], back shortly"; "[greeting] to the boys in [place]"; the plane/plain pun; "just touched down".
Frames you CAN use for the nonchalant voice: a voicemail, a text, a note on the fridge, "don't wait up", "save me a plate", "put it on the tab", "leave the key under the mat", "I'll be back for tea", "if anyone asks", "I'll ring you from the border". Invent your own.

## FLOW RULES (142 BPM, one bar = one line, about 1.7 seconds)
- 4 stresses per line, roughly even; the rhyme word lands on the 4th stress. Usually 8-12 syllables; 5-7 for a short plain line; up to 14 only if fast and light.
- You are writing a CHAIN: 8 bars that all end on one sound (soft endings and near-rhymes are fine). Shape it as a 3-bar run, ONE short plain breath line (5-7 syllables, still ending on the sound), then a 4-bar run. Each run goes: bland setup, flat report, closer on the rhyme word. Put a rhyme or echo INSIDE at least half the bars as well (mark internal rhymes with *asterisks*).
- Mix the bar shapes: no run of lines with the same opening.
- "mate" at most once in your whole batch; never as filler.
- Talk like a man: verbs, "I", "so", "then". Say each bar aloud at speed; if you stumble, rewrite.

## BANNED
Gag structures (setup then punchline); ending on a tag word that names the joke ("Dedication."); self-applause; arithmetic; journey, fairytale, vibes, legend, king, throne, neon, shadows, echoes; groaner puns (de-Nile, top banana, in the pink, so aisle be back, any port in a storm); real artist, film or brand names (describe instead); heavy swearing (cheeky is fine); instructional drug detail (deadpan imagery only); itinerary recap or holiday logistics; anything about Joe's looks, hair, relationships or job.
FACTS: use only your FACT BOX. Any other claim about Joe, or any invented crime-boss flourish: put (invented) after the bar.
