## The standard (from Joe, who knows the original best)
"There's not really any jokes at all, the comedy comes from the characters and wordplay."
- C (CHARACTER = nonchalance): the narrator reports something big, illicit, dangerous or absurd in a flat errand or domestic voice, often to an ordinary person in his life. Model: the original's line about being away in a rich desert city smoking weed and sending word to his wife that he'll be back shortly. Funny because he is so relaxed. No punchline, no wink, no explaining.
- W (WORDPLAY): clever and interesting, a second meaning found on a second listen. Model: the original cashing "lights out" into a type of bulb. The rhyme scheme and flow are what sell it.
A bar that is a gag (setup then punchline), a stand-up one-liner, a pun that announces itself, a holiday recap, or just a list of places is NOT what we want.

## What the original actually does (Genius line-by-line pass; use it as a yardstick)
- Its nonchalant lines narrate an extraordinary act in an everyday British admin or consumer register (parcel delivery, a pub chain, a schoolboy telling-off, cigarette prices), usually as a 2-4 line run with a bland setup line and a bathetic closer on the rhyme beat. The narrator never reacts to danger; violence is euphemised as logistics or games.
- Its wordplay cashes a stock phrase into a literal thing (lights out -> bulb, wrist slap -> banknote), the payoff word sits IN THE RHYME SLOT (the last word), and the payoff often lands on the next bar. Reward bars whose payoff word is the rhyme word.
- A domestic person (a wife, Mum) is usually MENTIONED, not addressed; the narrator mostly talks to the audience or his mates. Do not penalise a bar for not addressing someone.
- A bar can be one step of a 2-3 bar bit; judge it in the context of its chain, but still say if it stands alone.
- About a third of the original is accurate references (places, slang, brands). A correct local word is a plus.

## Score every bar
- L (landing), 0-3: 3 = I'd smile, rewind, or quote it; 2 = fine, would stay; 1 = flat, generic or filler; 0 = groan, cringe, or sounds written by an AI.
- F (flow), 0-3: 3 = scans cleanly at 142 BPM (4 beats, rhyme on beat 4, a satisfying match, nothing to trip on); 2 = OK; 1 = stumbles or the rhyme is padding; 0 = unspeakable.
- Reason: 6 words max.
- CLONE flag: if it copies the original's templates ("tell her I'm out in X doing Y, back shortly", "greeting to the boys in X", the plane/plain pun, "just touched down").
- If the bar is typed W or CW: is the second meaning REAL and NEAT (Y/N)?
- If typed C or CW: is it genuinely nonchalant, i.e. big thing + flat report (Y/N)?
